🍼 Guide for parents and babysitters

Babysitter rates in Amsterdam: pay, rules and how to choose

What sitters in Amsterdam ask per hour, the legal minimum for every age, the Dutch rules when someone babysits at your home, and how to pick a person you feel good about.

💶 What a babysitter costs in Amsterdam

  • An experienced adult babysitter in Amsterdam usually asks around €20 per hour.
  • On RaisingAmsterdam in November 2026, most babysitters who state a price ask between €15 and €23 per hour.
  • More than half of the babysitters write that the rate is to be agreed with the parents. That is normal here: the price depends on the number of children, their ages, the hours and how far the sitter travels.
  • A rate under €15 is below the legal minimum for a babysitter of 21 or older (see the table below). Lower rates are for younger sitters, such as students of 16 to 20.
  • Expect to pay more for a baby, for three or more children, for late nights and for a request on the same day.
  • Agree the rate before the first evening, and also what happens when you come home later than planned.

⚖️ The legal minimum per age

  • A babysitter who works for your family is covered by the Dutch minimum wage. The amount depends on age and is the same for every kind of work.
  • On top comes 8% holiday pay (vakantiegeld). Many families simply include it in the hourly rate.
  • The amounts change every 1 January and 1 July, so check the official table when you read this later.

🏠 The rules when someone babysits at your home

  • The Netherlands has a simplified scheme for private households: the Regeling dienstverlening aan huis. Babysitting (oppassen op de kinderen) is named in it.
  • It applies when the sitter works for your family on fewer than 4 days a week. The number of hours does not matter: one hour counts as a day. The sitter may also look after your children at their own home.
  • What you owe the sitter: at least the minimum wage plus 8% holiday pay, paid holidays (4 times the weekly hours per year), continued pay during illness for up to 6 weeks, and a safe place to work.
  • What you do not have to do: under this scheme you do not have to arrange anything with the tax office (Belastingdienst).
  • What the sitter should know: under this scheme the sitter is not insured for illness, disability or unemployment. A voluntary insurance is possible through UWV. The sitter reports the income to the Belastingdienst.
  • From 4 days a week with the same family the scheme stops, and it becomes regular employment with more obligations for you.
  • You can put your agreements in a short written contract. It avoids misunderstandings about pay, holidays and cancelling.

🧮 No childcare allowance for a babysitter

  • The childcare allowance (kinderopvangtoeslag) is only for registered childcare: a daycare centre (KDV), after-school care (BSO) or a registered childminder (gastouder).
  • A private babysitter is not registered childcare, so you pay the full hourly rate yourself.
  • If you need many fixed hours every week, compare the cost with daycare or a gastouder, where the allowance can pay a large part.

💛 How to choose: the first meeting

  • Meet first, at home, with your child there. Half an hour is enough. Watch how the sitter greets your child: do they get down to the child's height, do they wait, or do they take over?
  • Do one trial hour while you are at home. You will see more in that hour than in any interview.
  • Questions that tell you a lot:
    • Which ages have you looked after, and for how long?
    • What do you do when a child cries for a parent at bedtime?
    • What would you do if my child fell and hit their head?
    • Have you done a child first aid course (kinder-EHBO)?
    • How do you get home at night, and until what time can you stay?
  • Write things down for the evening: your phone numbers, the address, bedtime routine, allergies, what your child may eat and watch, and the GP's number.
  • Trust what you see. A child who is relaxed with the sitter after ten minutes tells you more than a perfect CV.

🔎 VOG and references: what you can check

  • A VOG (Verklaring Omtrent het Gedrag) is the Dutch certificate of conduct. It shows that a person's record is no objection for a certain kind of work.
  • The babysitter applies for it personally. At the municipality (gemeente) it costs €41.35. When an employer or organisation starts the application, it costs €33.85.
  • You can ask whether a sitter has a recent VOG or is willing to apply. Some families offer to pay the fee.
  • A VOG says nothing about experience or how someone is with children. Ask for one or two families you can call, and call them.
  • On RaisingAmsterdam, listings can show reviews from members and references from families the sitter has worked for. We do not check or employ the babysitters ourselves, so meeting and checking is always your step.

🙋 For babysitters: your rate and your listing

  • Know your minimum. If you are 21 or older, a family may not pay you less than €14.99 per hour (from 1 July 2026), plus 8% holiday pay. The table above shows every age.
  • Say your rate clearly. Parents answer a listing with a price sooner than one without. If you prefer to agree it, say what it depends on.
  • Write what parents look for: the ages you have experience with, your languages, the neighbourhoods you can reach, and the days and hours you are free.
  • Collect references. Families you have worked for can leave you a reference through a link, without making an account.
  • Remember that under the household scheme you are not insured for illness or unemployment, and that you report your income yourself.
  • A listing on RaisingAmsterdam is free for babysitters.

❓ Quick answers

  • How much does a babysitter cost in Amsterdam? An experienced adult sitter usually asks around €20 per hour. Most sitters on RaisingAmsterdam who state a price ask €15 to €23.
  • What is the minimum for an adult sitter? €14.99 per hour from 1 July 2026, plus 8% holiday pay.
  • Do I become an employer? Under 4 days a week the simplified household scheme applies, with no paperwork for the tax office.
  • Can I get kinderopvangtoeslag? No, only for registered childcare.
  • Can I ask for a VOG? Yes. The sitter applies personally, €41.35 at the municipality.
  • How do I find an English-speaking sitter? Filter the listings by language. In November 2026, 25 of the 29 babysitters list English.
Good to know: this is friendly orientation, not legal or tax advice. Wage amounts and rules change, so always check the official source linked in each section before you act on it.
